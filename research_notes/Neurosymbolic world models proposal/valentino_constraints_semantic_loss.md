## Q1. Dr Marco Valentino: role and key papers on LLM + formal logic / theorem provers, explanation-based NLI, autoformalisation, and reasoning in LLMs

### Takeaway
Dr Marco Valentino is "Lecturer in Artificial Intelligence & Applications of AI" in the NLP group of Sheffield's School of Computer Science. He was previously in the Neuro-Symbolic AI Group at the Idiap Research Institute and did his PhD at the University of Manchester. His central line of work puts an LLM in a loop with a symbolic theorem prover or logical solver. The LLM autoformalises natural-language explanations, the prover checks their logical validity, and its feedback drives refinement. A second line measures and controls how LLMs reason, separating formal validity from content plausibility (content effects in syllogisms) with activation steering and mechanistic interpretability.

### Inferences
- Valentino's method is mostly **post-hoc verify-and-refine**. An external symbolic engine (theorem prover or solver) checks a finished LLM output and sends feedback, and the model's weights or decoding distribution are not changed. The applicant's verifier labels generated definitions as supported, contradicted or unknown against the KB. It is a lightweight, non-prover analogue of the verification half of Explanation-Refiner, but without autoformalisation and without the refinement loop. That makes the refinement loop a natural PhD extension.
- The applicant's semantic loss (training-time) and hard logit mask (decoding-time) act at points Valentino's published pipelines mostly leave alone: inside training and inside the sampler. The closest Valentino work to these is the differentiable ILP solver (LREC-COLING 2024) and the symbolically-guided process supervision (EMNLP 2025). The proposal can therefore frame the applicant's work as complementary: constraints enforced during generation plus verification after it.
- The content-effects line, separating formal validity from content plausibility, is directly relevant to world models. A model that predicts plausible but physically invalid next states shows the manipulation analogue of a content effect.
- I found no Valentino paper on robotics, world models or planning. Linking his work to manipulation is an inference the proposal must make itself, not a citation.

## Q2. State of the art in enforcing symbolic constraints on neural/LLM outputs, and known limitations

### Takeaway
Training-time methods include semantic loss, its autoregressive extension the pseudo-semantic loss, DeepProbLog and Logic Tensor Networks. They shape the distribution but give no test-time guarantee. Decoding-time methods, such as grammar-constrained decoding and hard masks, guarantee validity but distort the model's distribution and can hit dead ends. Both families can satisfy constraints "for the wrong reason" (reasoning shortcuts). Exact constraint probability under an autoregressive model is #P-hard.

### Cited Findings

**Semantic loss and extensions**
- **Xu, J., Zhang, Z., Friedman, T., Liang, Y., & Van den Broeck, G. (2018). "A Semantic Loss Function for Deep Learning with Symbolic Knowledge." ICML 2018, PMLR 80:5502–5511.** "From first principles, we derive a semantic loss function that bridges between neural output vectors and logical constraints." — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/xu18h.html). PMLR renders the last author as "Guy Broeck".
- **Ahmed, K., Chang, K.-W., & Van den Broeck, G. (2023). "A Pseudo-Semantic Loss for Autoregressive Models with Logical Constraints." NeurIPS 2023 (Advances in NeurIPS 36).** Under autoregressive distributions, "computing the likelihood of even simple constraints is #P-hard". The method optimises the constraint's likelihood under a pseudolikelihood-based local approximation "centered around a model sample". — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/3accfe8332366a6f740d8740cd4cd653-Abstract.html); arXiv [2312.03905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03905)
- PSL's LLM experiment is the constraint closest to the applicant's banned-token loss. On GPT-2 (124M) with a toxic-word list, toxicity probability fell from 34.11% to 9.80%, and perplexity rose from 25.85 to 28.14 (Table 3). The paper also notes that setting toxic-word probabilities to zero (a hard mask) sends perplexity to infinity. — [arXiv 2312.03905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03905). These figures were transcribed in earlier local notes (`research_notes/Semantic loss original paper/xu_2018_semantic_loss.md`) and were not re-checked in this pass.

**Neuro-symbolic frameworks**
- **Manhaeve, R., Dumančić, S., Kimmig, A., Demeester, T., & De Raedt, L. (2018). "DeepProbLog: Neural Probabilistic Logic Programming." NeurIPS 2018 (spotlight).** A probabilistic logic programming language with "neural predicates", trainable end-to-end. — [arXiv 1805.10872](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.10872)
- **Badreddine, S., d'Avila Garcez, A., Serafini, L., & Spranger, M. (2022). "Logic Tensor Networks." Artificial Intelligence 303, 103649.** A differentiable, many-valued first-order "Real Logic" for learning and reasoning. — [arXiv 2012.13635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13635)

**Constrained decoding**
- **Geng, S., Josifoski, M., Peyrard, M., & West, R. (2023). "Grammar-Constrained Decoding for Structured NLP Tasks without Finetuning." EMNLP 2023 (Main).** Formal, including input-dependent, grammars constrain LM outputs. On extraction, disambiguation and parsing, the constrained models beat unconstrained ones and sometimes beat task-specific finetuned systems. — [arXiv 2305.13971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13971)
- **Park, K., Wang, J., Berg-Kirkpatrick, T., Polikarpova, N., & D'Antoni, L. (2024). "Grammar-Aligned Decoding." NeurIPS 2024.** "GCD techniques (and in general constrained decoding techniques) can distort the LLM's distribution." — [arXiv 2405.21047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21047)
- PICARD (Scholak et al., EMNLP 2021) is a schema-aware hard mask that raised T5-Base Spider exact match / execution accuracy from 57.2 / 57.9 to 65.8 / 68.4. Its beam search still "exited without finding a valid SQL prediction" on about 2% of T5-3B predictions. — [arXiv 2109.05093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05093). These figures come from earlier local notes (`research_notes/KB hard logit masks in transformers/`) and were not re-checked in this pass.

**Limitations**
- **Reasoning shortcuts.** Marconato, E., Teso, S., Vergari, A., & Passerini, A. (2023). "Not All Neuro-Symbolic Concepts Are Created Equal: Analysis and Mitigation of Reasoning Shortcuts." NeurIPS 2023. Neuro-symbolic models can reach high accuracy using concepts whose meaning differs from the intended one, which undermines compliance, generalisation and interpretability. — [arXiv 2305.19951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19951)
- **No test-time guarantee from losses.** Loss-based methods "cannot guarantee that the predictions will be consistent at test time" (Ahmed et al. 2022, Semantic Probabilistic Layers, NeurIPS 2022). — [arXiv 2206.00426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00426). Quote taken from earlier local notes and not re-checked in this pass.
- **Intractability.** Exact semantic loss is #P-hard for autoregressive models, hence PSL's local approximation. — [arXiv 2312.03905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03905)
- **Distribution distortion and dead ends** from hard decoding constraints (Grammar-Aligned Decoding; PICARD's failed beams, above).

### Inferences
- The applicant's design combines a semantic-loss-style term over the KB-allowed next-token set with a hard mask at decoding. That pairs a method with no test-time guarantee with one that guarantees validity but distorts the distribution, which is a defensible "both halves" design. Each component's known failure mode (shortcuts for the loss; distortion and dead ends for the mask) should be stated as a limitation.
- The applicant's per-step loss acts only where the KB fires on the current prefix. That is a local, token-level constraint, weaker than the sequence-level constraints PSL targets. This "constraints only act where they fire" limitation follows from the design and is not a literature finding.
- For world models, the analogue of a "forbidden next token" is a physically or logically impossible next state. The same trade-offs (soft loss vs hard mask, shortcuts) should carry over. This is an extrapolation.

- I did not re-verify the PICARD and SPL figures; they come from earlier local notes.

## Q3. Applications of constraint methods to planning and robotics

### Takeaway
In robotics, symbolic constraints on LLM or neural policies appear as four patterns. (a) Guided decoding of plans, where LM token probabilities are combined with grounded feasibility models. (b) Formal-logic safety filters that prune actions violating LTL constraints. (c) Translation to a formal planner (PDDL). (d) Differentiable probabilistic-logic "shields" inside policy-gradient RL. These are the decoding-time, mask-like, verifier-like and loss-like analogues of the applicant's three components.

### Cited Findings
- **Huang, W., Xia, F., Shah, D., Driess, D., Zeng, A., Lu, Y., Florence, P., Mordatch, I., Levine, S., Hausman, K., & Ichter, B. (2023). "Grounded Decoding: Guiding Text Generation with Grounded Models for Embodied Agents." arXiv 2303.00855.** Selects action sequences that score well under both the LM and grounded models of the environment, treated like probabilistic filtering. This is constrained decoding of robot plans. — [arXiv 2303.00855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00855). Venue UNVERIFIED: the arXiv page lists none, and I believe it appeared at NeurIPS 2023 but did not confirm this.
- **Yang, Z., Raman, S. S., Shah, A., & Tellex, S. (2024). "Plug in the Safety Chip: Enforcing Constraints for LLM-driven Robot Agents." ICRA 2024**, per the [project page](https://yzylmc.github.io/safety-chip); ICRA proceedings entry not directly confirmed. A queryable module based on linear temporal logic converts natural-language restrictions into formal constraints, explains violations, and prunes unsafe actions. It was tested in VirtualHome and on a real robot, and the project page reports a 100% safety rate with expert-verified LTL formulas. — [arXiv 2309.09919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09919)
- **Yang, W.-C., Marra, G., Rens, G., & De Raedt, L. (2023). "Safe Reinforcement Learning via Probabilistic Logic Shields." IJCAI 2023, pp. 5739–5749.** Probabilistic Logic Policy Gradient (PLPG) expresses safety constraints as differentiable probabilistic-logic shields, usable with any policy-gradient algorithm. It is reported to yield safer, higher-reward policies than other shielding methods. — [arXiv 2303.03226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03226); venue and pages from [researchr.org](https://researchr.org/publication/YangMRR23) via search snippet.
- **Liu, B., Jiang, Y., Zhang, X., Liu, Q., Zhang, S., Biswas, J., & Stone, P. (2023). "LLM+P: Empowering Large Language Models with Optimal Planning Proficiency." arXiv 2304.11477.** Translates a natural-language planning problem to PDDL, solves it with a classical planner, and translates the result back, yielding optimal plans on most benchmark problems where LLMs alone often fail. — [arXiv 2304.11477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11477). Venue UNVERIFIED (none listed on arXiv).

### Inferences
- The mapping to the applicant's components: hard logit mask ≈ Safety Chip's action pruning and Grounded Decoding's feasibility-weighted decoding; semantic loss ≈ PLPG's differentiable shield in the training objective; KB verifier ≈ LLM+P / Explanation-Refiner style hand-off to a formal engine. This gives the proposal a clean transfer story from text generation to manipulation planning.
- None of these papers enforce constraints on a learned **world model's** predicted next state, as opposed to the policy's chosen action. That looks like the gap a "neurosymbolic world model" project would fill. This inference rests on the limited sample above, not on a systematic search.
