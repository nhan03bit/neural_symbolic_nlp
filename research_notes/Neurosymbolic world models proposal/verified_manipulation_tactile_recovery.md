## Q1. Dr Amir Ghalamzan (Esfahani): affiliation and key works on tactile sensing, slip prediction/control, predictive tactile models, agri-robotics

### Takeaway
Ghalamzan is currently at the University of Sheffield: Senior Lecturer (Associate Professor) in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, CMI Theme Lead for Robotics and Autonomous Systems, and lead of the Intelligent Manipulation Lab. Before that he was at the University of Surrey, and earlier he published from Lincoln. His core line of work, which fits the project's objective 2 directly, is **action-conditioned tactile prediction**: learned forward models that predict future tactile signals from planned robot actions. He uses these models (a) to predict slip and (b) inside receding-horizon or predictive controllers that change the trajectory to avoid slip, or to manipulate strawberry clusters.

## Q2. How recent systems detect execution failures and recover; their limits

### Takeaway
The main LLM-based recovery pattern is closed-loop: perception outputs (success detectors, scene descriptions, multisensory summaries) are written into text, an LLM explains the failure or replans, and the robot re-executes. These systems work at the level of language and symbols. They rely on perception or success-detection modules, and none of the verified examples uses tactile or force predictions to *anticipate* a failure before it happens.

### Cited Findings
- **Inner Monologue: Embodied Reasoning through Planning with Language Models**, Wenlong Huang et al., CoRL 2022 (PMLR 205, pp. 1769–1782), arXiv:2207.05608. [VERIFIED via the CoRL anthology plus arXiv]. The authors "continually add information from various feedback sources into the language model prompts": success detection, scene description and human interaction. No extra training is needed. Closed-loop feedback improved high-level instruction completion in simulated and real tabletop rearrangement and in real kitchen mobile manipulation. — [arXiv:2207.05608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05608); [CoRL entry](https://mlanthology.org/corl/2022/huang2022corl-inner)
  - Limit: some feedback sources were human-provided or oracle, and full automation of feedback is listed as future work. This came from a secondary summary; check the paper. — [project page](https://innermonologue.github.io)
- **REFLECT: Summarizing Robot Experiences for Failure Explanation and Correction**, Zeyi Liu, Arpit Bahety, Shuran Song, CoRL 2023 (PMLR 229), arXiv:2306.15724. [VERIFIED]. It builds a three-level hierarchical summary from RGB-D, audio and robot state. An LLM reasons over this summary to explain failures, and the explanation guides a language-based planner to correct the failure. It introduces the RoboFail dataset. — [arXiv:2306.15724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15724); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/liu23g.html)
  - Reported accuracy (88.4% sim vs 68.8% real for execution-failure explanation) comes from a secondary review. UNVERIFIED; check the paper's tables before quoting. — [Liner review](https://liner.com/review/reflect-summarizing-robot-experiences-for-failure-explanation-and-correction)
  - Limit (from the design): REFLECT processes "post-execution" observations, so it explains failures after they happen rather than predicting them.
- **Code as Policies: Language Model Programs for Embodied Control**, Jacky Liang, Wenlong Huang, Fei Xia, Peng Xu, Karol Hausman, Brian Ichter, Pete Florence, Andy Zeng. arXiv:2209.07753 (Sep 2022; v4 May 2023). The arXiv page lists no venue; it is commonly cited as ICRA 2023, which was not verified here. LLMs write Python policies with loops and conditionals that call perception and control APIs, giving reactive controllers and waypoint tasks. Hierarchical code generation recursively defines undefined functions. — [arXiv:2209.07753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07753)
- **TouchWorld: A Predictive and Reactive Tactile Foundation Model for Dexterous Manipulation**, Jianyi Zhou et al. (12 authors), arXiv:2607.07287 (Jul 2026), no venue listed. [VERIFIED arXiv]. This is a hierarchical, tactile-aware recovery system. A planner splits the task and predicts *tactile subgoals*. A policy produces nominal action chunks from vision and touch, and a fast refinement policy applies residual corrections from recent tactile and proprioceptive feedback "such as slip or force mismatch". It reports 65.0% success in clean settings and 53.7% under human perturbations on six long-horizon contact-rich tasks, +15.7 and +18.5 pp over the strongest baseline. — [arXiv:2607.07287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07287)

### Inferences
- In LLM-based recovery (Inner Monologue, REFLECT, Code as Policies), the error signal is a discrete success or failure observation turned into text. There is no formal guarantee and no prediction of contact failures (e.g. slip) before they occur. A predictive tactile model plus symbolic constraint checking would address this gap.
- TouchWorld shows that tactile subgoal prediction plus fast residual correction is now being explored (2026). However, it uses learned (not symbolic or verifiable) planners, which leaves room for a neurosymbolic version.

### Gaps
- **Behaviour trees with execution monitoring** and **classical TAMP execution monitoring** were not verified in this session. No citation is given; search e.g. Colledanchise & Ögren's behaviour-tree book, or "PDDLStream" (Garrett et al.), if needed.
- No verified quantitative comparison of failure-detection accuracy across methods.

## Q3. Formal methods for runtime constraint verification or enforcement in robot learning, and combinations with learned or foundation-model policies

### Takeaway
Three verified families are relevant. (1) **Shields** synthesised from temporal-logic specifications restrict or correct RL actions. (2) **Control barrier function (CBF) safety layers** are now attached to VLA foundation models. (3) **STL specifications** are produced by LLMs and solved by a trajectory optimiser. For contact and tactile constraints specifically, the Ghalamzan group's constrained receding-horizon slip controller is the closest verified example of MPC-style runtime constraint enforcement with a learned predictor.

### Cited Findings
- **Safe Reinforcement Learning via Shielding**, Mohammed Alshiekh, Roderick Bloem, Ruediger Ehlers, Bettina Könighofer, Scott Niekum, Ufuk Topcu. arXiv:1708.08611 (2017); the arXiv page lists no venue (widely cited as AAAI 2018, not verified here). A reactive "shield" synthesised from a temporal-logic specification either supplies the set of safe actions before the agent chooses, or monitors the chosen action and corrects it only if it would violate the specification. The paper analyses conditions that preserve the learner's convergence. — [arXiv:1708.08611](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611)
- **VLSA: Vision-Language-Action Models with Plug-and-Play Safety Constraint Layer**, Songqiao Hu, Zeyi Liu, Shuang Liu, Jun Cen, Zihan Meng, Xiao He. arXiv:2512.11891 (v1 Dec 2025; v2 Jul 2026). [VERIFIED arXiv; no venue seen]. "AEGIS" adds a plug-and-play CBF-based safety constraint layer to existing VLA models. The authors claim "theoretical guarantees" while keeping instruction following. It introduces the SafeLIBERO benchmark and reports about 59% better obstacle avoidance and about 17% higher task success than baselines. Caveat: the abstract does not state the assumptions behind the guarantees, and results are on the authors' own simulated benchmark. — [arXiv:2512.11891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11891v1)
- **AutoTAMP: Autoregressive Task and Motion Planning with LLMs as Translators and Checkers**, Yongchao Chen, Jacob Arkin et al., ICRA 2024 (venue per the official repo), arXiv:2306.06531. [VERIFIED/PARTIAL]. An LLM translates natural language to Signal Temporal Logic (STL), and a planner then "directly optimizes the whole trajectory, i.e., task and motion planning together". STL was chosen over LTL for more expressive constraints. Errors are corrected by a syntax checker plus autoregressive LLM semantic re-prompting. It outperforms direct LLM planning on tasks with hard geometric and temporal constraints. — [arXiv:2306.06531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06531); [GitHub](https://github.com/yongchao98/AutoTAMP)
- MPC with learned tactile constraints: Nazari et al. (arXiv:2209.06019, Q1 item 3) use a learned action-conditioned slip predictor inside a constrained optimiser and receding-horizon controller to keep predicted slip within limits. — [arXiv:2209.06019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06019)
- Other 2026 CBF-plus-VLA filters (UNVERIFIED, snippet only): an "attention-guided" training-free CBF safety filter for VLAs (arXiv:2606.09749), and "Safe-Night VLA" with CBF runtime layer and thermal perception (arXiv:2603.05754). — [arXiv:2606.09749](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09749); [arXiv:2603.05754](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05754.pdf)

### Inferences
- The verified CBF and shield work enforces *geometric or collision* constraints. None of the verified examples enforces *contact or tactile* constraints (slip, force limits) with formal guarantees. Bringing STL or CBF-style specifications over *predicted tactile states* is a plausible novelty gap for the proposal. This is an inference, not a verified absence; a targeted search for "tactile STL" or "slip CBF" is recommended.

### Gaps
- Runtime monitors for STL robustness on learned policies (e.g. online STL monitoring in manipulation) were not verified this session.
- No verified survey of safety filters for learned manipulation was retrieved.

## Q4. Coupling high-level symbolic planning with low-level learned contact/tactile dynamics across multiple timescales

### Takeaway
Verified work splits into two parts. One part uses learned tactile or visuo-tactile dynamics models inside MPC (RoboPack, deep-FPC). The other uses hierarchical learned planners with fast tactile correction loops (TouchWorld). AutoTAMP couples symbolic (STL) specifications with trajectory optimisation, but without tactile dynamics. No verified paper combines **symbolic planning + learned tactile/contact dynamics + formal constraint checking + automated recovery** in one multi-rate architecture, which is the gap objective 2 targets.

### Cited Findings
- **RoboPack: Learning Tactile-Informed Dynamics Models for Dense Packing**, Bo Ai, Stephen Tian, Haochen Shi, Yixuan Wang, Cheston Tan, Yunzhu Li, Jiajun Wu. RSS 2024, arXiv:2407.01418. [VERIFIED]. A recurrent graph neural network estimates object state (particles plus latent physical properties) from past visual and tactile observations and predicts future states. The model is used with model-predictive control on a real robot with a soft tactile sensor. It needs about 30 minutes of data per task and adapts online. — [arXiv:2407.01418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01418)
- **TouchWorld** (Q2): a three-level hierarchy (subtask planner with tactile subgoals, then nominal policy, then fast tactile residual refinement). It is an explicit multi-timescale design where "most existing policies treat touch as a slow input" is the stated motivation. — [arXiv:2607.07287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07287)
- **Deep-FPC** (Q1 item 4): learned tactile forward model inside an online functional predictive controller. — [arXiv:2303.05393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05393)
- **AutoTAMP** (Q3): symbolic/temporal-logic task specification coupled with a continuous trajectory optimiser. — [arXiv:2306.06531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06531)
- Secondary or snippet-only (UNVERIFIED): **VLA-Touch** (2025) reportedly uses tactile input at two levels, tactile-to-language for the planner and diffusion-based action refinement. **CoRAL** (RSS 2026 per project page) uses an LLM as a cost designer for an MPPI sampling-based planner, with online system identification for contact-rich tasks. **LEMMo-Plan** (arXiv:2409.11863) plans sequential contact-rich tasks from multimodal (tactile, F/T) demonstrations with LLMs. — [CoRAL project](https://sites.google.com/view/lira-coral); [LEMMo-Plan arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11863)

### Inferences
- A plausible architecture for the proposal follows from these sources. A slow symbolic or STL planner (like AutoTAMP) sets subgoals and constraints. A medium-rate predictive controller rolls out a learned action-conditioned tactile model (like Nazari/Mandil, deep-FPC, RoboPack) and checks the constraints on predicted tactile states (like Nazari's constrained optimiser, or CBF/shield ideas). A fast reactive loop corrects slip (like TouchWorld's refinement). A predicted constraint violation would trigger symbolic replanning (like Inner Monologue or REFLECT, but before the failure rather than after).

### Gaps
- The VLA-Touch, CoRAL and LEMMo-Plan details were not verified on primary pages.
- No verified paper on a formally verified multi-rate (hierarchical) controller using tactile prediction.
- Neurosymbolic world models specifically (e.g. learned symbolic abstractions over tactile states) were not searched in this session.
