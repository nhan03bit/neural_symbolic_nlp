## Q1. Main approaches that combine LLMs/VLMs with symbolic planners or formal world models: claims and documented limits

### Takeaway
The literature has converged on a few patterns. In each, the foundation model acts as a translator, modeller or predicate-proposer, while a sound symbolic planner or verifier guarantees correctness. These patterns are: (a) LLM translates the task into PDDL and a classical planner solves it; (b) LLM builds or repairs the PDDL *domain* model with validator or human feedback; (c) LLM-Modulo generate-and-test loops with external verifiers; (d) predicates and operators (abstract world models) invented from robot data with VLM/LLM help; (e) hierarchical models that predict symbolic and visual transitions together. The recurring documented limit is the translation/grounding step. LLM-generated formal models are usually syntactically valid but often semantically wrong, and multimodal problem generation still yields valid plans only around half the time.

### Cited Findings
**(a) LLM as translator to PDDL, classical planner solves**
- LLM+P converts a natural-language planning problem into a PDDL problem file, calls a classical planner, and translates the plan back. The authors report that "LLM+P is able to provide optimal solutions for most problems, while LLMs fail to provide even feasible plans for most problems." The framing premise is that "LLMs cannot reliably solve long-horizon planning problems." — [Liu et al., arXiv:2304.11477, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11477)
- Limit of LLM+P as stated in its abstract: it takes a natural-language *problem* description and produces the problem PDDL. (In the paper the domain PDDL is supplied; I recall this from the paper body and did not re-check it in this session, so treat that detail as UNVERIFIED.) — [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11477)
- ViLaIn (ICRA 2024) extends translation to the multimodal case. It generates PDDL *problem descriptions* from a language instruction plus a scene observation, using LLMs and VLMs, and refines them from symbolic-planner error messages. Reported result: ">99% syntactically correct problems" but "valid plans with more than 58% accuracy." — [Shirai et al., arXiv:2311.00967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00967)
- AutoTAMP (ICRA 2024) translates language into an intermediate formal task representation (signal temporal logic in the paper) for a task-and-motion planner. It "automatically detect[s] and correct[s] both syntactic and semantic errors via autoregressive re-prompting." The authors argue that subgoal factorisation is "untenable" when "complex environmental and temporal constraints are involved." — [Chen et al., arXiv:2306.06531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06531)
- Planetarium (NAACL 2025) exposes the semantic-correctness gap in translation. For GPT-4o, "96.1% of the PDDL problem descriptions generated ... are syntactically parseable, 94.4% are solvable, but only 24.8% are semantically correct." It introduces a PDDL-equivalence algorithm because validators alone cannot detect plausible but wrong PDDL. — [Zuo et al., arXiv:2407.03321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03321)

**(b) LLM constructs or corrects the domain (world) model**
- Guan et al. (NeurIPS 2023) explicitly frame PDDL as an "explicit world (domain) model" that sound planners use. Because "LLMs may not generate a fully functional PDDL model initially," the LLM serves as "an interface between PDDL and sources of corrective feedback, such as PDDL validators and humans." Result: GPT-4 produced "high-quality PDDL models for over 40 actions," and the corrected models solved 48 planning tasks. They criticise LLM-as-planner as impractical because of "limited correctness of plans" and reliance on simulator/environment feedback. — [Guan et al., arXiv:2305.14909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14909)
- Yu et al. (TMLR 2025): "directly generating PDDL domains with current LLMs remains an open challenge due to the lack of PDDL training data." They use Best-of-N sampling plus verbalised-ML refinement and report "over 50% success rate" on NL-to-domain and problem-to-domain generation. This is still only roughly half. — [Yu et al., arXiv:2502.04728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04728)
- Silver et al. (AAAI 2024) have GPT-4 synthesise Python *generalized planners* for PDDL domains, with automated debugging against training tasks. They find GPT-4 "a surprisingly powerful generalized planner," that "automated debugging is very important," and that "just two training tasks are often sufficient." — [Silver et al., arXiv:2305.11014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11014)
- WorldCoder builds a Python-program world model from interaction and defines reward "optimism as a logical constraint between a program and a planner." It is reported as more sample-efficient than deep RL and more compute-efficient than ReAct-style agents, but evaluation is on gridworlds and task planning, not physical robots. — [Tang et al., arXiv:2402.12275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12275)
- Survey framing: Tantakoun et al. position "LLMs as tools for formalizing and refining planning specifications to support reliable off-the-shelf AP planners," and "identify critical challenges and future directions." — [Tantakoun et al., arXiv:2503.18971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18971)

**(c) LLM-Modulo / verifier-in-the-loop**
- Kambhampati et al. (ICML 2024 position paper) argue that "auto-regressive LLMs cannot, by themselves, do planning or self-verification," but should be used as "universal approximate knowledge sources." They propose "LLM-Modulo Frameworks" that combine LLMs "with external model-based verifiers in a tighter bi-directional interaction regime," where the verifiers' models can themselves be "acquired with the help of LLMs." — [Kambhampati et al., arXiv:2402.01817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01817)
- Text2Motion (Autonomous Robots 2023) is a robotics instance. Language-based planning is checked for geometric feasibility of skill sequences, framed as producing "feasible plans" from instructions (the title claim). — [Lin et al., arXiv:2303.12153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12153)
- **[BACKGROUND, pre-2023]** SayCan grounds LLM proposals with skill value functions ("affordances"). It motivates the field by noting that LLMs "lack real-world experience, which makes it difficult to leverage them for decision making within a given embodiment." — [Ahn et al., arXiv:2204.01691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01691)
- Supervisor-relevant analogue in NLP (not robotics): Explanation-Refiner couples an LLM with an external theorem prover. It autoformalises explanations into logic, checks entailment, and uses prover feedback to refine them. This shows the same verifier-in-the-loop neurosymbolic pattern for natural-language reasoning. — [Quan, Valentino et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.172.pdf) (details from search snippets; paper not opened in this session)

**(d) Learning symbolic predicates and abstract world models from data with foundation-model help**
- VisualPredicator (ICLR 2025 Spotlight) introduces "Neuro-Symbolic Predicates, a first-order abstraction language that combines the strengths of symbolic and neural knowledge representations," with "an online algorithm for inventing such predicates and learning abstract world models." Compared with hierarchical RL, VLM planning and symbolic predicate invention across five *simulated* robotic domains, it reports "better sample complexity, stronger out-of-distribution generalization, and improved interpretability." — [Liang et al., arXiv:2410.23156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23156)
- ExoPredicator (ICLR 2026) extends this to dynamic worlds. It "jointly learns (i) symbolic state representations and (ii) causal processes for both endogenous actions and exogenous mechanisms" (e.g., water heating, dominoes). Learning uses "variational Bayesian inference combined with LLM proposals," and it reports generalisation "to held-out tasks with more objects and more complex goals" across five simulated tabletop environments. — [Liang et al., arXiv:2509.26255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26255)
- UniPred (IEEE T-RO, Foundation Models for Robotics special issue) explicitly names the top-down vs bottom-up split. Prior methods learn predicates "top-down, by prompting foundation models without data grounding, or bottom-up, from demonstrations without high-level priors." UniPred uses LLM-proposed predicate effect distributions to supervise neural predicate classifiers on visual foundation features. It reports "2-4 times higher success rates than top-down methods and 3-4 times faster learning than bottom-up approaches" across five simulated and two real-robot domains, and supports "symbolic models beyond STRIPS assumptions." — [Wang et al., arXiv:2512.17992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17992)

**(e) Neurosymbolic world models that predict symbolic state transitions**
- H-WM (2026) "jointly predicts logical and visual state transitions by combining a high-level logical world model with a low-level visual world model." The predicted logical actions and latent visual subgoals are fed into VLA policies as "intermediate state guidance." The authors report it "consistently improves VLA's performance by stabilizing long-horizon execution and mitigating error accumulation" on three long-horizon benchmarks and real robots. It also releases LIBERO-Logic, which pairs observations and robot states "with logical actions and predicate-based logical states." — [Huang et al., arXiv:2602.11291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11291)

### Inferences
- Across (a) to (e), the symbolic component supplies soundness and long-horizon structure, while the foundation model's errors concentrate at the language/vision-to-formal interface. That interface is the natural target for the DLA's objective 1 ("combining multimodal foundation models with formal logical reasoning and symbolic planners").
- The ~24.8% semantic correctness (Planetarium, text-only), ~58% valid plans (ViLaIn, multimodal) and ~50% domain-generation success (Yu et al.) together suggest that verifying *semantic* faithfulness of generated formal models remains open. This holds especially for multimodal input, which is exactly the manipulation setting. These numbers come from different benchmarks and are not directly comparable.
- Predicate-invention work (VisualPredicator, ExoPredicator, UniPred) is the closest prior art to a "neurosymbolic world model for manipulation." It is mostly evaluated in simulation, with UniPred adding two real-robot domains.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the exact LLM+P experimental assumption that the domain PDDL is human-provided against the paper body (UNVERIFIED in this session).
- I did not find a head-to-head benchmark comparing paradigms (a) to (e) on the same manipulation tasks.
- VLM-planner-plus-simulator verification (e.g., physics-simulator checking of VLM plans) was not covered by a verified primary paper in this session beyond Text2Motion and AutoTAMP.

## Q2. What "world model" means in recent robotics/ML, and where symbolic structure or logical constraints have been added

### Takeaway
In current ML/robotics, a "world model" is a learned internal simulator of environment dynamics used for imagined or counterfactual rollouts. Examples include latent-dynamics RL models such as DreamerV3 and large video "world foundation models" such as NVIDIA Cosmos. A 2025 survey names long-horizon temporal consistency, error accumulation and physical (rather than pixel) consistency as core open problems. Symbolic structure has been added mainly by (i) learning abstract symbolic world models (predicates and operators/processes) for planning, (ii) using code or PDDL as the world model, and (iii) pairing a logical world model with a visual one (H-WM).

### Cited Findings
- Survey definition: "World models serve as internal simulators that capture environment dynamics, enabling forward and counterfactual rollouts to support perception, prediction, and decision making." The proposed three-axis taxonomy covers functionality (decision-coupled vs general-purpose), temporal modelling, and spatial representation (global latent vector, token feature sequence, spatial latent grid, decomposed rendering). — [Li et al., arXiv:2510.16732, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16732)
- Open challenges named by that survey: "scarcity of unified datasets," "the need for evaluation metrics that assess physical consistency over pixel fidelity," the performance vs real-time-control efficiency trade-off, and "the core modeling difficulty of achieving long-horizon temporal consistency while mitigating error accumulation." — [Li et al., arXiv:2510.16732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16732)
- Latent-dynamics (Dreamer-style): DreamerV3 "learns a model of the environment and improves its behavior by imagining future scenarios," outperforming specialised methods "across over 150 diverse tasks, with a single configuration." It was the first to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch. — [Hafner et al., arXiv:2301.04104, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)
- Video/foundation world models: NVIDIA positions a "world foundation model as a general-purpose world model that can be fine-tuned into customized world models" for Physical AI. The platform comprises video curation, pretrained models, post-training examples and video tokenizers, released open-weight. — [NVIDIA, arXiv:2501.03575, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03575)
- H-WM explicitly critiques purely neural world models. Existing approaches "mainly rely on visual, latent, or language prediction, which can be difficult to ground in executable robot actions and prone to compounding errors over long horizons," whereas TAMP's "compact symbolic representations of world transitions" support long-horizon reasoning but lack "synchronized visual prediction." — [Huang et al., arXiv:2602.11291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11291)
- Symbolic abstract world models: VisualPredicator ("learning abstract world models" with neuro-symbolic predicates) — [arXiv:2410.23156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23156). ExoPredicator (abstract world models with endogenous and exogenous causal processes; "each causal process models the time course of a stochastic cause-effect relation") — [arXiv:2509.26255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26255). UniPred ("Symbolic world models help by decomposing tasks into discrete predicates that capture object properties and relations") — [arXiv:2512.17992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17992).
- PDDL as world model: Guan et al. call the PDDL domain an "explicit world (domain) model" — [arXiv:2305.14909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14909). Yu et al. describe generating "a symbolic world model where classic searching algorithms, such as A*, can be seamlessly applied" — [arXiv:2502.04728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04728).
- Code as world model with logical constraint: WorldCoder defines optimism "as a logical constraint between a program and a planner." — [arXiv:2402.12275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12275)
- LLMs as transition models are evaluated directly. Embodied Agent Interface benchmarks four LLM modules, including "transition modeling," with fine-grained error types ("hallucination errors, affordance errors, various types of planning errors"). — [Li et al., NeurIPS 2024 D&B, arXiv:2410.07166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07166)

### Inferences
- Two communities use "world model" differently. ML/robot learning (Dreamer, Cosmos) means learned neural dynamics in latent or pixel space. Planning/neurosymbolic work means a symbolic transition model (PDDL operators, predicates, causal processes, code). A proposal can frame "neurosymbolic world model" as the bridge between them, with H-WM as the closest recent instance.
- The survey's open problems (long-horizon consistency, error accumulation, physical consistency metrics) map directly onto what symbolic transition models offer: discrete abstract states that do not drift, and checkable preconditions and effects. This is an inference, not a claim made by the survey.

### Gaps
- I did not find (and so do not claim) a verified paper that imposes formal logical constraints, such as temporal-logic or physics-law constraints, *inside the training objective* of a large video world model like Cosmos. This may exist but was not verified in this session.
- DreamerV3's journal publication (reportedly Nature, 2025) was not verified here; cite the arXiv version.

## Q3. Evidence that purely data-driven foundation-model policies (VLAs: RT-2, OpenVLA, π0) fail on logical consistency, long-horizon or physical-grounding requirements, and what neurosymbolic approaches claim to fix

### Takeaway
VLAs report strong generalist manipulation results. However, 2025–2026 evaluations show (i) collapse under simple perturbations, attributed to memorisation (LIBERO-PRO: >90% falls to 0.0%), and (ii) poor performance and no generalisation on structured long-horizon tasks compared with a PDDL-based neuro-symbolic system (Tower of Hanoi: 34% vs 95%; 0 vs 78% on unseen 4-block), at far higher training energy. Separately, LLM planning benchmarks show that standalone LLMs and reasoning models remain unreliable planners. Neurosymbolic approaches claim to restore reliability, data efficiency, OOD generalisation, interpretability and long-horizon stability.

### Cited Findings
**What VLAs claim (baseline for comparison)**
- RT-2 expresses robot actions as text tokens and co-fine-tunes VLMs on robot and web data. It reports "significantly improved generalization to novel objects" and "emergent capabilities" such as interpreting commands absent from robot training data. — [Brohan et al., arXiv:2307.15818, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15818)
- OpenVLA is a 7B open-source VLA trained on 970k real-world demonstrations. It reports beating RT-2-X (55B) "by 16.5% in absolute task success rate across 29 tasks." — [Kim et al., arXiv:2406.09246, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09246)
- π0 is a flow-matching VLA built on a pretrained VLM. Its abstract itself acknowledges "major obstacles in terms of data, generalization, and robustness" for generalist robot policies, and evaluates tasks such as laundry folding and box assembly. — [Black et al., RSS 2025, arXiv:2410.24164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24164)

**Documented failures**
- LIBERO-PRO perturbs objects, initial states, instructions and environments. Models "achieve over 90% accuracy under the standard LIBERO evaluation, [but] their performance collapses to 0.0% under our generalized setting." The authors attribute this to "rote memorization of action sequences and environment layouts." Models "persist in executing grasping actions when the target object is replaced with irrelevant items," and outputs are unchanged under "corrupted instructions or even messy tokens." A search summary indicates OpenVLA, π0 and π0.5 were evaluated (I did not check the model list against the paper body). — [Zhou et al., arXiv:2510.03827, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03827)
- The Price Is Not Right (ICRA 2026) compares fine-tuned π0 head-to-head with "a neuro-symbolic architecture that combines PDDL-based symbolic planning with learned low-level control" on Towers of Hanoi in simulation. Results: 3-block, 95% vs 34% (best VLA). Unseen 4-block, the neuro-symbolic system gets 78% "whereas both VLAs fail to complete the task." VLA fine-tuning "consumes nearly two orders of magnitude more energy." Caveats: simulation only, a single VLA family, and a highly structured puzzle task. — [Duggan et al., arXiv:2602.19260, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19260)
- H-WM motivates its design by VLA long-horizon "error accumulation" and reports that adding logical and visual world-model guidance "consistently improves VLA's performance by stabilizing long-horizon execution." — [Huang et al., arXiv:2602.11291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11291)

**LLM-planning evidence (non-embodied, but motivates symbolic planners)**
- PlanBench: "on many critical capabilities—including plan generation—LLM performance falls quite short, even with the SOTA models." Most claims of LLM planning are on commonsense tasks "where it becomes hard to tell whether LLMs are planning or merely retrieving." — [Valmeekam et al., NeurIPS 2023 D&B, arXiv:2206.10498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10498)
- On o1: "while o1's performance is a quantum improvement on the benchmark ... it is still far from saturating it," and it raises "questions about accuracy, efficiency, and guarantees." — [Valmeekam et al., arXiv:2409.13373, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13373)
- 2025 survey conclusion: "LLMs are not well-suited to serve as standalone planners" given long-horizon limitations, but are valuable "when combined with other approaches." — [Aghzal et al., arXiv:2502.12435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12435)

**What neurosymbolic approaches claim to fix**
- Reliability, data efficiency and energy efficiency on long-horizon tasks: "emphasizing the role of explicit symbolic structure in improving reliability, data efficiency, and energy efficiency." — [Duggan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19260)
- Sample complexity, OOD generalisation and interpretability vs HRL and VLM planning: [VisualPredicator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23156)
- Generalisation to more objects and more complex goals, plus fast planning: [ExoPredicator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26255)
- Correctness guarantees from sound planners, with fewer per-plan human corrections: [Guan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14909); optimal plans where LLMs fail: [LLM+P](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11477)
- Long-horizon stability and reduced error accumulation for VLAs: [H-WM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11291)

### Inferences
- The strongest *direct* VLA-vs-neurosymbolic evidence (Duggan et al.) uses a single, highly structured task in simulation. A proposal should cite it as indicative, not conclusive, and can position the PhD as testing such claims on contact-rich, less structured manipulation.
- LIBERO-PRO's failure mode (ignoring instruction content, acting on memorised layouts) is a logical-grounding failure. Explicit symbolic state with checkable preconditions is the obvious candidate remedy, but no paper found here demonstrates that remedy on LIBERO-PRO.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the conference venues of RT-2 or OpenVLA in this session (commonly cited as CoRL 2023 and CoRL 2024; treat as UNVERIFIED until checked).
- No verified paper in this session quantifies VLA failures specifically on *logical consistency* (e.g., contradiction of stated constraints) as opposed to robustness or long-horizon success.
- ScienceDaily press coverage (Apr 2026) of Duggan et al. gives "~1% training energy / ~5% execution energy" figures. These are not in the abstract and were not verified against the paper, so do not cite them.

## Q4. Open problems named in recent surveys of neurosymbolic AI for robotics and planning

### Takeaway
Surveys converge on several problems: (i) LLMs cannot be standalone long-horizon planners, so integration architectures (formalisers, LLM-Modulo) are the research frontier; (ii) faithful formalisation and grounding of formal models from language and perception; (iii) world models need long-horizon consistency, physical-consistency metrics and real-time efficiency; (iv) neurosymbolic AI overall under-invests in explainability, trustworthiness and meta-cognition. I found no dedicated 2025–2026 survey specifically on "neurosymbolic robotics" that I could verify.

### Cited Findings
- Aghzal et al. (2025) advocate "a balanced methodology that leverages the inherent flexibility and generalized knowledge of LLMs alongside the rigor and cost-effectiveness of traditional planning methods." They highlight long-horizon reasoning as the main LLM weakness. — [arXiv:2502.12435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12435)
- Tantakoun et al. (2025) note that LLMs "often struggle with long-horizon planning problems requiring structured reasoning," driving neuro-symbolic integration. They say that "identifying optimal AP deployment frameworks can be daunting and introduces new challenges," and enumerate "critical challenges and future directions" for LLM-as-formaliser. The specific challenge list was not extracted in this session. — [arXiv:2503.18971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18971)
- Li et al. (2025, world models for embodied AI) name these open challenges: unified datasets, "evaluation metrics that assess physical consistency over pixel fidelity," the performance/real-time trade-off, and "long-horizon temporal consistency while mitigating error accumulation." — [arXiv:2510.16732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16732)
- Colelough & Regli (2025, PRISMA review of 167 papers from 2020–2024): research is concentrated in learning and inference (63%), knowledge representation (44%), and logic and reasoning (35%). "Explainability and trustworthiness are less represented (28%), with Meta-Cognition being the least explored area (5%)." "Significant gaps remain in explainability, trustworthiness, and Meta-Cognition." — [arXiv:2501.05435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05435)
- Acharya et al. (IEEE TAI, 2023) taxonomise neurosymbolic RL and planning into "Learning for Reasoning, Reasoning for Learning and Learning-Reasoning" and "identify research opportunities and challenges." This predates foundation-model robotics, so use it as background framing. — [arXiv:2309.01038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01038)
- Kambhampati et al. (ICML 2024) frame the integration problem itself: "rather than simply pipelining LLMs and symbolic components," tighter bi-directional LLM-verifier interaction is needed, and verifier models should be learned with LLM help. — [arXiv:2402.01817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01817)
- Primary papers name these open problems in their motivations: top-down (ungrounded FM prompting) vs bottom-up (prior-free) predicate learning ([UniPred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17992)); exogenous and concurrent processes not captured by action-centric abstractions ([ExoPredicator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26255)); the lack of synchronised symbolic and visual prediction ([H-WM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11291)); validators that cannot verify semantic intent ([Planetarium](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03321)); scarce PDDL training data ([Yu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04728)).

### Inferences
- A defensible open-problem statement for the proposal, synthesised from the above: *how to learn, ground and verify symbolic transition models from multimodal foundation-model outputs, so that they stay semantically faithful, physically consistent over long horizons, and are usable by sound planners in contact-rich manipulation.* This combines the formaliser gap (Planetarium, Yu, Tantakoun), the grounding gap (UniPred, ViLaIn), the world-model consistency gap (Li et al. survey, H-WM) and the explainability/trust gap (Colelough & Regli).
- Valentino's LLM-plus-theorem-prover line (Explanation-Refiner) supplies a methodological bridge: autoformalisation plus prover-feedback refinement could transfer to verifying LLM/VLM-generated symbolic world models. This is an inference about fit, not a published result.

### Gaps
- No verified 2025–2026 survey dedicated to *neurosymbolic robotics* (rather than LLM planning or world models generally) was found. An "Ugur et al., Neuro-Symbolic Robotics" overview surfaced in search, but its date and venue are UNVERIFIED and it is not cited.
- The detailed challenge lists inside the Tantakoun and Aghzal surveys were not extracted; only abstract-level claims are reported.
- A "World Model for Robot Learning: A Comprehensive Survey" (reported as arXiv:2605.00080) surfaced only via a third-party summary. It is UNVERIFIED and not cited.
