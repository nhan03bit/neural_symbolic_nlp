## Q3. How do the figures show a real example passing through the structure?

### Takeaway
In the reference, the path is shown by colour identity, not by the edges. One green callout on the query and one green outcome are the only marked elements, and every edge looks the same. So the figure shows the outcome and its evidence, and leaves the route to the reader.

- NS Fig. 4: [V] the only structure fill that differs is `#94C47D` on the "Suicidal Ideation (Y)" ellipse, and the same `#94C47D` fills the "Suicidality Concept" callout and the condition highlight in "Response Constraints." — [NS p.6](../../docs/paper/Neurosymbolic_Artificial_Intelligence_Why_What_and_How.pdf#page=6).

### Inferences
- Green means "reached or fired for this input", so a single colour is enough because it marks one thing.
- To keep the reference's look and also resolve the route, the two options with precedent are a one-line path in text (Roy) or pruning untaken branches (Sheth Fig. 4). A one-line path under the formula disturbs the drawing least. Colouring the taken edges would be a new choice with no precedent in these figures; it might still help, but that is a design decision for the owner.

### Gaps
- None of the papers explains its marking convention. All the readings above come from the drawings.

## Q6. What makes the rejected attempt read as generic next to the reference?

### Takeaway
The rejected figure already copies the surface features (ellipses, dashed edges, a dashed panel and a thick arrow), so those are not what is missing. It reads as generic because it explains a system instead of showing one input going through it.
